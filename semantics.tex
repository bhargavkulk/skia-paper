\section{Semantics}

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \begin{minipage}{0.40\linewidth}
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{S S}
      Image  & : \; Point $\to$ Pixel \\[2pt]
      Shape  & : \; $\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{Point})$ \\[2pt]
      Blend  & : \; Pixel $\to$ Pixel $\to$ Pixel \\[2pt]
      Filter & : \; Pixel $\to$ Pixel
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{%
      Our abstract model consists of
      \textsf{Images} and \textsf{Shapes}
      over abstract types \textsf{Pixels} and \textsf{Colors},
      which can be blended together
      to form images.
    }
    \label{fig:model}
  \end{minipage}
  \hfill
  \begin{minipage}{0.58\linewidth}
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{S c T}
      Layer & ::= & Empty() \\
            & |   & Draw(l: \textsf{Layer}, s: \textsf{Shape},\\
            &     & \hspace{4em}p: \textsf{Paint}, c: \textsf{Shape})\\
            & |   & BlendLayer(l\textsubscript{bot}: \textsf{Layer},
                    l\textsubscript{top}: \textsf{Layer},\\
            &     & \hspace{8em}p: \textsf{Paint}) \\[2pt]
      Paint & ::= & Paint(fill: Image, blend: Blend)
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{}
    \label{fig:layer}
  \end{minipage}

Our semantics of Skia is built around
  a simple abstract model of images and shapes,
  shown in \Cref{fig:model}.
Under these semantics,
  rendering occurs by taking \textsf{Shapes},
  filling them with primitive \textsf{Images},
  and then blending the results with a \textsf{Blend} together in various orders
  to yield more complicated \textsf{Images}.
Our semantics also introduces a field for \textsf{Paints} not seen in \Cref{sec:skia}
  in Skia called \textsf{Filters},
  which are basically transforms over \textsf{Pixel}.

However, Skia does not permit
  arbitrary drawing and blending operations;
  instead, these always happen in a prescribed order.
We thus introduce the language of \textsf{Layers},
  shown in \Cref{fig:layer},
  which defines the core \texttt{Draw} and \texttt{BlendLayer} operations
  in Skia.
We focus on these two operations specifically
  because they are the core target of the optimizing compiler
  described in \Cref{sec:optimize}.
The \textsf{Layer} constructors can be defined in terms of the abstract model,
  as shown in \Cref{fig:denote}.

\begin{figure}
  \begin{align*}
    \denote{\texttt{Empty()}}(\textsf{pt})
    &= \mathsf{Transparent}\\[2pt]
    \denote{\texttt{Draw(l, s, p, c)}}(\mathsf{pt})
    &= \mathtt{blend_p}\left(\denote{\mathtt{l}(\mathsf{pt})},\;
       \begin{cases}
         \mathtt{fill_p}(\mathsf{pt}) & \mathsf{pt} \in \mathtt{s} \cap \mathtt{c} \\
         \mathsf{Transparent} & \textsf{otherwise}
       \end{cases}
       \right)\\[2pt]
    \denote{\texttt{BlendLayer(l\textsubscript{bot}, l\textsubscript{top},
    p)}}(\mathsf{pt})
    &= \mathtt{blend_p}\left(\denote{\mathtt{l_{bot}}}(\mathsf{pt}),\;
      \begin{cases}
        \alpha_\mathsf{px}\cdot\denote{\mathtt{l_{top}}}(\mathsf{pt}) & \forall
                                                                   \mathsf{pt},
                                                                   \mathtt{fill_p}(\mathsf{pt})
                                                                   = \mathsf{px}
        \\
        \denote{\mathtt{l_{bot}}}(\mathsf{pt}) & \textsf{otherwise}
      \end{cases}
      \right)
  \end{align*}
\end{figure}

We now define a grammar for the Skia drawing API in the figure below.

\begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{S M c T}
    Command & c & ::= & Draw($g$, $p$) \\
            &   & | & DrawText(string, $p$) \\
            &   & | & Clip($cl$, Intersect | Difference) \\
            &   & | & Save(); $c$; Restore() \\
            &   & | & SaveLayer($p$); $c$; Restore() \\
            &   & | & $c$; $c$ \\[1pt]
    Geometry & g,\;cl & ::= & Rect($\ldots$) | RRect($\ldots$) | Path($\ldots$) \\[2pt]
    Paint & p & ::= & Paint($f$, $st$, $c_f$, $b$) \\[2pt]
    Fill & f & ::= & Color(Pixel) | LinearGradient($\ldots$) | $\ldots$ \\[2pt]
    Filter & f & ::= & Id | Luma | $\ldots$ \\[2pt]
    BlendMode & b & ::= & SrcOver | DstIn | Src | $\ldots$\\
  \end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Our grammar for Skia simplifies certain aspects of the actual Skia API:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The Skia API has separate \texttt{Draw} and \texttt{Clip} commands for
      each \textsf{Geometry},
      and not the overloaded function we present in the grammar.
    \item The Skia API does not have structured constructs for \texttt{Save} and
      \texttt{SaveLayer}.
    \item Many of the arguments of the constructor have been elided as they are uninmportant.
\end{enumerate}

More crucially, we have omitted certain effects and commands in Skia.
The \texttt{Style} tells Skia whether to fill or stroke the shape.
It can be considered as a function over \texttt{Geometry},
  basically modifying it.
Similiarly,
  \texttt{MaskFilters} allow us to implement Gaussian blurs.
This could also be understood as functions over \texttt{Geometry},
  and omitting them does not affect our semantics at all.

Skia also provides facilities to transform, rotate, and perform other affine
  transformations.
It is well known that these transformations can be done using
  matrix multiplications.
So Skia maintains a transform state along with the layer and clip state.
The \texttt{Concat44} command multiplies a 4x4 matrix with the transform state,
  and affect the drawing of all commands after.
The transform state as a whole can be removed by multiplying
  the transform state of a command into the geometry
  and the clip, which is what we do.

Finally,
  we also omit image filters from our discussion of semantics.
Image filters are the final way to transform a layer prior to blending.
When a paint has an image filter,
  all drawing happens in a seperate pixel buffer,
  and the effect defined by the filter is applied to the entire buffer
  before its blended.
Image filters seem similiar to color and mask filters,
  but they allow more complicated effects,
  as they can act on groupd of pixels instead of single pixels.

To wrap this section up,
  we define an operational semantics for the Skia API.
We first define a step relation $\longmapsto$:

\begin{align*}
\longmapsto&:\textsf{Command} \times \Sigma \to \Sigma
\end{align*}

$\Sigma$ is the canvas state:

\begin{align*}
\Sigma&:\textsf{Layer} \times \textsf{Geometry} \times \Sigma
\end{align*}

The canvas state has a reference to another canvas state,
  emulating a stack of layers and clips.

The following rules, define $\longmapsto$ over each command on \textsf{Command}.

\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule
  { }
  {
    \langle
    \texttt{Draw(g, p)},
    \langle L, C, K \rangle
    \rangle
    \;\longmapsto\;
    \langle
    \texttt{Draw(L, g, p, C)},
    C,
    K
    \rangle
  } \and
  \inferrule
  { }
  {
    \langle
    \texttt{Clip(C', Intersect)},
    \langle L, C, K \rangle
    \rangle
    \;\longmapsto\;
    \langle
    L, C \cap C', K
    \rangle
  } \and
  \inferrule
  { }
  {
    \langle
    \texttt{Clip(C', Difference)},
    \langle L, C, K \rangle
    \rangle
    \;\longmapsto\;
    \langle
    L, C / C', K
    \rangle
  } \and
    \inferrule
  {
    \langle
    \texttt{c},
    \langle L, C, \langle L, C, K \rangle \rangle
    \rangle
    \;\longmapsto\;
    \langle
    L', C', \langle L, C, K \rangle
    \rangle
  }
  {
    \langle
    \texttt{Save(); c; Restore()},
    \langle L, C, K \rangle
    \rangle
    \;\longmapsto\;
    \langle
    L', C, K
    \rangle
  } \and
 \inferrule
 {
    \langle
    \texttt{c},
    \langle \texttt{Empty()}, C, \langle L, C, K \rangle \rangle
    \rangle
    \;\longmapsto\;
    \langle
    L', C', \langle L, C, K \rangle
    \rangle
  }
  {
    \langle
    \texttt{SaveLayer(p); c; Restore()},
    \langle L, C, K \rangle
    \rangle
    \;\longmapsto\;
    \langle
    \texttt{BlendLayer(L, L', p)}, C, K
    \rangle
  }
\end{mathpar}

\noindent
$\longmapsto^*$ is the reflexive transtive closure of $\longmapsto$. We can then
define a evaluation function for \textsf{Command} to \textsf{Layer}.

\begin{gather*}
\textsf{eval} : \textsf{Command} \to \textsf{Layer} \\
\textsf{eval}(c) = L,\;\langle c,\; \langle \texttt{Empty()}, \mathcal{U}, \varnothing
                   \rangle \rangle \longmapsto^* \langle L, C, \varnothing \rangle
\end{gather*}

denote(eval(c')) = denote(eval(c))
