\begin{abstract}
Browser engines require fast rasterization libraries
  to quickly and efficiently draw application pixels to the screen
  and update those pixels for animations and user interactions.
Skia is the most common such library,
  used in all major browser engines as well as
  Android and the Flutter mobile application framework.
Yet using Skia efficiently is difficult
  due to Skia's complex semantics
  and the need to deeply understand its execution model.
Even highly-optimized applications like Google Chrome
  consistently generate suboptimal Skia commands,
  leading to slower rasterization that necessary.

To address this issue,
  we introduce a new formal semantics of Skia,
  including its command layer,
  a functional layer focused on drawing and blending,
  and an abstract model of shapes and colors.
To demonstrate the utility of this semantics,
  we use the semantics to verify rewrites
  that optimize common sub-optimal patterns
  in Skia commands generated by Google Chrome.
We further develop a Skia optimizer
  based on these commands,
  which can produces traces for translation validation
  in our semantics.
On a collection of \grmetalnumbench Skia programs
  gathered from the top 100 websites,
  the compiler improves rasterization time on \grmetalnumspmatch,
  with speedups ranging from \grmetalspmatchmin to \grmetalspmatchmax
  while compilation time is at most \grmetalmaxopttime.
The optimizer also generalizes
  across Skia back-ends and new websites.
\end{abstract}
